Your heart has four rooms. The two at the top are separated by a thin wall called the **septum**. An atrial septal defect means that wall didn't finish closing — so there's an opening between them.

Blood that has already been to the lungs slips back through the hole and goes around again. Nothing is leaking out of your body. Nothing is torn. **Your heart is doing extra laps, not falling apart.**

Many people carry one for decades without knowing. It's often found by accident, or because the extra work finally started to show up as breathlessness, tiredness or a fluttering heartbeat.

Whether it needs closing depends on how big it is, where it sits, and whether the right side of your heart has started to stretch. That's a real decision with real criteria — **not a coin toss, and not an emergency you have to solve tonight.**
