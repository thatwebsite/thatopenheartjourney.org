In plain words

What an ASD actually is

Your heart has four rooms. The two at the top are separated by a thin wall called the septum. An atrial septal defect means that wall didn't finish closing — so there's an opening between them.

Blood that has already been to the lungs slips back through the hole and goes around again. Nothing is leaking out of your body. Nothing is torn. Your heart is doing extra laps, not falling apart.

Many people carry one for decades without knowing. It's often found by accident, or because the extra work finally started to show up as breathlessness, tiredness or a fluttering heartbeat.

Whether it needs closing depends on how big it is, where it sits, and whether the right side of your heart has started to stretch. That's a real decision with real criteria — not a coin toss, and not an emergency you have to solve tonight.

Where it sits
Between the left and right atria — the two upper chambers.
Common types
Ostium secundum (most common), ostium primum, sinus venosus, coronary sinus.
How it's found
Usually an echocardiogram. Sometimes a TEE, bubble study or cardiac MRI for detail.
How it's closed
A catheter-delivered device for suitable secundum defects. Open surgery — stitched or patched — for larger ones or other types.
What "patch" means
A small piece of your own pericardium or synthetic material, sewn over the opening. It becomes part of you.

The part nobody prepares you for

What to ask, when every doctor says something different

Getting several opinions isn't distrust — it's normal for heart surgery. These are the questions that turned a confusing pile of advice into a decision I could actually make.

How big is the defect, and what type is it?

Size in millimetres and the type both change what treatment is even possible.

Has the right side of my heart started to enlarge?

This is one of the main reasons closure gets recommended rather than watching.

Am I a candidate for device closure, or only open surgery?

If the answer is no, ask specifically why — usually it's the rims, the type, or the size.

What happens if I wait a year?

A good answer is specific about risk. A vague one tells you something too.

How many of these do you do a year?

Volume matters in cardiac surgery. It's a fair question and good surgeons expect it.

What does recovery look like, week by week?

You need this to plan work, family and money — not just to know the risks.

After

The weeks nobody photographs

The first days are intensive care, then a ward. You'll be asked to walk sooner than feels reasonable, and to hug a pillow when you cough. The breastbone is the slow part — it's bone, and bone takes its time. Expect weeks of no lifting, no pushing, no pulling yourself up with your arms.

Tiredness arrives in waves and lasts longer than you expect. So does a strange flatness of mood that a lot of people describe and almost nobody mentions beforehand. It is common, and it passes.

Cardiac rehab, where it's available, is worth every session. And the scar fades into something you stop noticing — right around the time you notice you can climb stairs without stopping.
